Why round one was too safe

I read "the creative layer must be deletable" as "the creative layer must be small." Those are different constraints.

Deletable governs architecture. It says nothing about how far the thing can go. So I built five sound bases with polite garnishes, and you were right to reject them.

Then I read the sources I had skipped, and found the three studios who actually do spectacular work saying the same thing, independently, for pure craft reasons:

"One or two key WebGL moments is the recipe, not the whole page."

field.io recipe

"One big set-piece is the recipe, not five small ones."

active theory recipe

"Fewer, better beats. The recipe needs a payoff scene."

resn recipe

"Design a static first frame that remains complete when JavaScript, media, WebGL or motion is unavailable."

Meng To · awwwards-quality bar

The award-grade standard itself demands the deletable layer. Ambition and deletability were never in tension. The mistake was building small instead of building singular. Every direction below spends its entire budget on one payoff and keeps the rest quiet.

D5 is dropped. You were right that a run trace is a thing we would only build because BeatMind exists.

D8 · the one I would build

Denoise

The site arrives as noise and resolves. Because that is literally what he trained a model to do.

step 0 / 2250
He trained the LoRA himself. Six-stage dataset pipeline, 2,250 steps.
Live canvas · real denoise curve
HomeWorkNotesStack
The idea
A diffusion model starts at pure noise and denoises into an image over N steps. He trained one. So the site's entire visual grammar is that process: type, captures and page transitions all resolve out of noise.
Why it is his
Nobody else can honestly use this. It is not a metaphor borrowed from space or nature, it is the actual mechanism of the work. That is divergence operator 6 done properly.
Graphics
One WebGL or canvas noise field. Captures denoise into place on scroll. The step counter on each project is its real number: 2,250 for the LoRA, 307 commits, 8 pipeline stages.

The payoff: first load. The hero resolves from noise in about 1.2s while the headline is already readable underneath. One set-piece, then the page goes quiet and behaves like a document.

Deletes cleanly: the static first frame is the resolved state. Kill the canvas and you have a normal, complete site. This is exactly Meng To's rule.

Watch: noise is expensive on a mid-range Android. Cap DPR, render at half resolution, pause offscreen, and swap for the resolved frame under prefers-reduced-motion.

D9 · the light one

Citation

Every claim on the site is footnoted to its evidence. Touch a claim and the source lights up.

What he actually did

Built and shipped three AI products in five months1 for a paying studio client, working alone.

Cut stem separation from 70s to 23.4s2 by root-causing a silent CPU fallback.

Predicted a 40% gain from a model swap, measured 8%, and reverted it3.

HomeWorkNotesStack
The idea
His whole thesis is evidence-bound systems that refuse to answer without a source. So the site behaves the way his software behaves. No claim stands unsourced, and the interaction proves it.
Why it converts
A sceptical founder's real question is "is any of this true." This is the only direction that answers it structurally rather than by asserting harder.
Graphics
Warm paper, editorial serif, a hairline that threads from claim to margin. On mobile the margin becomes an inline expansion under the claim.

The payoff: the moment a visitor realises every single number is clickable and nothing is decoration.

Deletes cleanly: without JS the footnotes are ordinary anchor links to a sources list. That is how footnotes worked before the web.

Watch: it demands that every claim really does have a source. Any unsourced line becomes conspicuous. That is a feature and it is also work.

The idea
Vivid decomposes a script into an N-shot sequence with world and character bibles. The contact sheet is the object that work produces, and it solves a real problem: it makes five screenshots feel like a body of work rather than a thin gallery.
Graphics
Sprocket rails, grease-pencil circles on the selected frame, shot numbers, a light-table glow on hover. Warm amber against a darkroom brown.

The payoff: the light table. Frames sit underexposed until you touch one, then it blooms to full colour. It rewards a cursor without requiring one.

Deletes cleanly: without JS or hover it is a grid of captioned images, which is a perfectly good work index.

Watch: the register is cinema, and his buyer is a SaaS founder. Charming, or off-brief. This is the direction most likely to split opinion.

D11 · the loudest

Kinetic

HomeWorkNotesStack
The idea
Turn the biggest weakness into the concept. He has no photography, and every recipe that needs it was ruled out. So refuse imagery entirely and spend the whole budget on kinetic type: weight and width animate on scroll along cubic-bezier(.83,0,.17,1).
Cost
Cheapest of the five to build and the fastest to load. One variable font file replaces an image system.
Each page
A different typographic composition rather than the same template recoloured. Notes runs narrow and quiet; Stack runs as a wide condensed list.

The payoff: the hero resolving as the axes settle. field.io's grammar, achieved with one font file instead of a WebGL pipeline.

Deletes cleanly: variable-font axes degrade to a static weight. Older browsers get a normal bold headline.

Watch: type-only sites can read as a student project if the writing is not excellent. This direction puts all the weight on the copy.

Where I land

D8, with D9's footnotes underneath it.

D8 is the only one here that no other person on earth can honestly ship. Everything else in this document is a good idea someone else could also have. A portfolio that denoises is available only to someone who trained the model, and he did, over 2,250 steps with a dataset pipeline he built.

It also satisfies the whole brief at once. It is spectacular, it is one set-piece rather than five, the static first frame is the finished site, and it costs one canvas.

D9 is the substrate, not a rival. Footnoting every claim is a content discipline, not a look, so it survives being bolted under any of these. Under D8 it does the convincing while the noise does the impressing.
