The frame

What every direction has to survive

The revert test

Build the clear site, ship it, then add one creative layer in its own commit. Revert it. If what remains is a working, complete site, you are safe. If not, the creative thing has become the information architecture again, which is exactly how v1 died.

Screenshots, not photography

Five products captured at two widths, three silent recordings, one portrait. No commissioned photography, no testimonials, no usage numbers. BeatMind has zero users and that stays on the page.

It must not read as generated

The detectors already flagged Inter and Geist Mono as overused faces on this very repo. Neither appears below. Nor does a 2×2 grid, a violet-to-blue gradient, or a centred-everything hero.

What it gets right
Hierarchy from scale, not weight. Every headline sits at 400, the same weight as body, and works purely through size and −0.04em tracking. Body at weight 200. No cards, no borders, no shadows anywhere. One filled violet pill per view, and never as a surface.

Risk 1, the ghost. Dala's own summary line is "constellation floating on black velvet." A particle constellation is the one visual v1 has already burned, and anyone who saw v1 will read it as the same site.

Risk 2, the company it keeps. Its own reference sheet lists Linear, Vercel, Anthropic and Runway as similar. Violet on pure black is the exact pattern flagged as the most recognisable generated look.

Risk 3, density 1. One or two elements per viewport, never information‑dense. That is D4's problem again: it needs imagery we do not have, and it fights twelve projects.

Worth stealing regardless: the weight‑200 body, hierarchy through scale alone, and the absolute refusal of cards and borders. Those three survive being moved to any palette.

Recommendation

D1 as the base, D5 as the creative layer

D1 + D5

They are the architecture the brief demands, already split. The Spec Sheet passes the ten-second test on text alone, which is the Phase 1 gate, and it survives having every moving thing removed because it was a document to begin with. The Run Trace is the one signature moment. It is animated, graphic, and unlike anyone else's site, and it lives in a section one commit can delete.

It also resolves the tension in the brief honestly. The ask is a site that is creative and animated; the constraint is that the creative layer must be deletable. This is the only pair here where the creative thing is genuinely additive rather than load-bearing.

If you want the Dala feeling, the honest route is not D6. It is D5 with D6's typographic discipline borrowed: hierarchy from scale rather than weight, no cards, no borders, and one accent used only on the one thing you want clicked. You get the weightlessness without inheriting the constellation or the company it keeps.

Before anything is locked

Four things I need from you

  1. Pick a direction, or tell me what is missing from all six.If none of these is it, that is a useful answer and we search again rather than settling.
  2. Refero captures.The Refero MCP needs a paid subscription, and the design-pass skill will not scrape the site because its robots.txt disallows every AI crawler. So the workflow is manual: browse it yourself, save what you like into design-kit/inbox/refero/ named brand--what-caught-your-eye.png. The suffix matters more than the prefix. Six captures is the useful number.
  3. Settle the BeatMind figures.The resume says 19 days, 194 commits, 27,000 lines. The case study says 24 days, 307 commits, ~30,500 lines. The case study is later and more precise, and the D1 specimen above uses it. One number, everywhere.
  4. Run the ten-second test on the sentence alone.No website. Say it to five people and write down what comes back, verbatim. If the sentence does not land as a sentence, none of these six saves it.
