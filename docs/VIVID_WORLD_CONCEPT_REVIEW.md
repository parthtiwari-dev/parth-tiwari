# Vivid world concept review

Written 2026-08-31. Status: **Story Loom review animatic implemented; owner storyboard
approval pending**. The owner authorized implementation after the three-board review. The
result remains a standalone study, not an Astro world, and does not change the Vivid
repository or move portfolio implementation ahead of `BUILD_PLAN.md`.

## Truth boundary

The current `upgrade-flux` branch was inspected before the concepts were made. Vivid's
verified product direction is a sequence of photographs that tells one story, with identity,
adherence, realism and speed evaluated per run. Its current FLUX.2-native style registry is
`None`, one experimental realism adapter and one pinned Turbo speed adapter. The older anime,
watercolour, oil-paint and comic LoRAs were FLUX.1 checkpoints and were deliberately removed
during the FLUX.2 migration.

Anime, watercolour, ink and stop-motion therefore appear in the concept review only as a
possible future style chapter. They are not current capability claims. They may enter the
published world only after real FLUX.2-compatible outputs exist and receive the same visual
and provenance review as the photographic sequence.

The three concept boards were generated from the real local `single_character` evaluation
inspection sheet: a four-shot Kyoto pottery sequence with one recurring ceramicist. The
boards are visual-development references, not Vivid outputs and not publishable evidence.
The selected Story Loom plate is stored under `design/references/vivid-story-loom/` solely for
the standalone review animatic; the two rejected boards were not copied into the portfolio.

## Shared narrative contract

Every direction tells the same project-specific story with native vertical scroll:

1. A written scene is separated into ordered shot beats.
2. A neutral character anchor establishes the person to preserve.
3. Reference bindings connect that identity to each shot without making every composition
   identical.
4. Successive frames resolve into one readable storyboard.
5. The failed previous-render conditioning experiment visibly copies a hand pose into the
   next shot.
6. The system returns to neutral character references rather than chaining rendered pixels.
7. The final contact sheet lets the visitor judge the sequence together.
8. The world ends in darkness with one explicit route to the paper case study.

Scrolling is the only required input. It advances a persistent graphic without trapping or
snapping the document. Reduced motion and no JavaScript receive the final contact sheet, the
failed comparison and the complete written account as stable frames.

## Direction A — Continuity Darkroom

One story develops across a physical contact-print laboratory. Script paper feeds into a
neutral portrait, four frames emerge under amber safelight, a chinagraph circle identifies
the bad frame, and visible reference threads reconnect the corrected sequence to the anchor.

- **Strength:** clearest explanation of the actual product and closest fit with Vivid's
  proposed contact-sheet interface.
- **Risk:** film-laboratory imagery is familiar; craft and real output quality must carry the
  distinction.
- **Production shape:** layered real images, film masks, CSS light/shadow and code-drawn
  reference lines. No 3D runtime is required.

## Direction B — Story Loom

Script beats, character traits and visual continuity become separate threads in one dark
wood-and-film loom. The neutral portrait is the master pattern. Frames are woven in order;
one over-tight previous-frame ribbon freezes a pose and receives an oxblood repair stitch.
The complete sequence leaves the loom as one story cloth.

- **Strength:** most ownable Vivid metaphor. It can explain binding, continuity and failure
  while remaining one continuous spatial machine.
- **Risk:** the loom must stay attached to real plan/reference fields so it does not become
  decorative fantasy. The optional multi-style strip must wait for real compatible outputs.
- **Production shape:** real frames and references on the foreground; restrained code-drawn
  threads and a decorative pre-rendered loom behind them. Mobile uses fewer visible threads
  and closer crops rather than shrinking the desktop composition.

**Selected for implementation review:** the owner asked to begin implementation after this
direction was recommended. It communicates more of Vivid than denoising alone and is more
distinctive than a conventional contact sheet, while still permitting the contact sheet to
be the final judgement surface. The implementation and evidence are recorded in
`VIVID_WORLD_STUDY.md`.

## Direction C — Latent Cinema

The visitor descends through one monumental film gate while a frame moves from coarse latent
noise to subject, place, identity and texture. A side reference rail follows the protagonist
through the sequence. The failed copied-pose frame interrupts the descent before the final
storyboard opens in a dark cinema.

- **Strength:** strongest spectacle and closest evolution of the original `lock.html`
  denoise study.
- **Risk:** it makes model sampling the protagonist when Vivid's actual differentiator is
  multi-shot continuity. It is also the hardest direction to make equally strong on phone.
- **Production shape:** precomputed visual stages from a real selected run, CSS masks and
  Canvas 2D only if measured. Never fabricate intermediate diffusion frames from the final
  image and label them as real.

## Real build-time data contract

The final world must consume a reproducible, static export from one owner-approved Vivid
run. The export needs:

- source run identifier and model/profile provenance;
- scene beats and ordered shot identifiers;
- character-bible keys and the actual neutral reference images used;
- final shot images, prompts, seeds and per-shot inference settings;
- the actual style/adapter identifier for every image;
- real identity, adherence and realism results only where the evaluator produced them;
- one publishable failure artifact for the previous-render pose-copying account;
- rights/licence notes for the model and every public image.

The portfolio builds this export into static HTML and media. It performs no runtime Vivid
generation and does not imply that an exploratory concept board came from the product.

## Sequence that avoids rebuilding

1. Owner selects A, B or C and decides whether future multi-style exploration belongs in
   the first storyboard or remains omitted.
2. Vivid's active identity/reference phase reaches its own evidence gate; the portfolio does
   not pre-empt that repository plan.
3. Owner selects one publishable real story run and one real failure comparison.
4. Export and validate the static data contract above.
5. Produce a route storyboard at desktop and phone widths for owner approval.
6. Implement the chosen world only in the build-plan phase that owns project worlds, using
   the shared world engine proven by the flagship foundation.

## Owner decision

Review the implemented Story Loom scroll and approve, revise or reject it as Vivid's
storyboard direction. The implemented future-style chapter stays explicitly exploratory;
production still requires real compatible outputs before any style becomes a capability
claim.
